About me

I've always wanted to understand how things work.

That curiosity started with objects and tools, eventually led me to computer science and today shapes the way I approach software engineering: understand the problem, explore the system and find a practical way to improve it.

Luanda to Montréal

A move across continents, a new home and the beginning of a path shaped by curiosity and persistence.

Where it started

My story started long before software.

I was born in Luanda, Angola and moved to Montréal with my family when I was eight. Growing up, I watched my parents work hard to create better opportunities for our family. Their persistence shaped the way I approach my own goals: keep learning, work seriously and make the most of the opportunities I'm given.

As a kid, I was naturally curious about objects and tools. I liked figuring out how things worked and what I could do with them. Years later, a computer science class in high school gave that curiosity a new direction and eventually led me to study computer science at Collège Montmorency.

My first steps in tech

I didn't start as a developer.

My first professional experience in technology started with helping people solve problems. That eventually opened the door to building software of my own.

  • Technical support — Level 1

    I joined Revenu Québec through the Alternance Travail-Études program while studying computer science at Collège Montmorency. I started by helping employees over the phone and learning how technical problems affect real users.

  • Technical support — Level 2

    I later moved into Level 2 support, where I handled tickets and solved problems directly with users. The role gave me a better understanding of troubleshooting, communication and the systems behind the issues I was resolving.

  • My first developer role

    After telling my manager that I wanted to do more development work, he connected me with another team. That opportunity became my first professional software development role, where I used Angular to build web forms for internal service requests.

Becoming an engineer

I wanted to understand more than just the code.

After college, I chose to study software engineering at ÉTS because I wanted a broader understanding of how software is designed, built, tested, deployed and maintained in real systems.

Since then, my internships have given me the opportunity to work across several areas of software engineering and helped me discover where I want to keep growing.

Full-stack development

Working across frontend and backend systems taught me to think beyond individual components and understand how applications fit together as a whole.

Testing & reliability

My experience in QA and automated testing showed me that good software is not only about building features but also about making them dependable and maintainable.

Cloud & DevOps

Working with CI/CD pipelines, containers, Kubernetes, Terraform and cloud platforms pushed me to think about how software gets deployed, operated and secured.

AI & intelligent search

More recently, I explored AI/ML through RAG, embeddings, vector search and cloud-based retrieval systems for technical information.

Outside software

Guitar

Gaming

Where I'm heading

I'm still learning and that's exactly where I want to be.

Long term, I want to grow into an engineer who can take ownership of complex systems, make thoughtful technical decisions and help the people around me succeed.

I'm especially interested in the paths toward technical leadership and senior DevOps or platform engineering, while continuing to strengthen my foundations as a software developer.
